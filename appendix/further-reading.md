# Further Reading

A short, deliberately curated list of resources that go deeper than this
manual can. These are widely-regarded, real works — listed by title and
author so you can find them. No links (to avoid pointing you at a wrong
URL); search the title and author.

> This is a starting set, not a canon. The compounding-reading habit
> (see [../13-hidden-knowledge/compounding-reading.md](../13-hidden-knowledge/compounding-reading.md))
> matters more than any single book. Read a few of these, then follow
> your own thread.

## Craft & Career

| Title | Author(s) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *The Pragmatic Programmer* | Andrew Hunt, David Thomas | The classic on professional software judgment and habits |
| *A Philosophy of Software Design* | John Ousterhout | Short, opinionated, on managing complexity and good design |
| *Code Complete* | Steve McConnell | Exhaustive on construction-level craft |
| *The Mythical Man-Month* | Fred Brooks | Why software projects are hard; still true decades on |

## Reading, Refactoring & Legacy Code

| Title | Author(s) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Working Effectively with Legacy Code* | Michael Feathers | The source of seams, characterization tests, and the legacy mindset (see [../14-advanced/working-with-legacy-code.md](../14-advanced/working-with-legacy-code.md)) |
| *Refactoring* | Martin Fowler | The catalog and discipline of safe, incremental change |
| *The Art of Readable Code* | Dustin Boswell, Trevor Foucher | Practical, concrete on writing code for the reader |

## Systems & Scale

| Title | Author(s) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* | Martin Kleppmann | The definitive modern tour of distributed data systems (pairs with [../14-advanced/working-in-distributed-systems.md](../14-advanced/working-in-distributed-systems.md)) |
| *Site Reliability Engineering* | Google (eds. Beyer, Jones, Petoff, Murphy) | How a large org runs production; freely available online |
| *Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces* | Remzi & Andrea Arpaci-Dusseau | Excellent, free OS fundamentals |

## Ongoing Inputs (Not Books)

Habits matter more than any title — these are the *kinds* of things worth
following (find the specific ones in your ecosystem):

- **Changelogs and release notes** of the dependencies you rely on —
  read what you upgrade into (see [../13-hidden-knowledge/compounding-reading.md](../13-hidden-knowledge/compounding-reading.md)).
- **Public incident postmortems** — companies publish them; collections
  exist. The cheapest way to learn failure modes (see
  [../14-advanced/incident-response.md](../14-advanced/incident-response.md)).
- **Recent conference talks** by maintainers of projects you use — the
  hidden roadmap and the philosophy (see [../13-hidden-knowledge/conference-talks.md](../13-hidden-knowledge/conference-talks.md)).
- **RFC / design-doc repos** of projects you follow — watch real
  decisions get made.
- **The source of your dependencies** — when something surprises you,
  read *why* in the code rather than guessing.
- **A high-signal newsletter or two** in your language/domain — curation
  beats the firehose.

## How to Approach This List

- **Don't binge.** One book absorbed beats five skimmed. Apply what you
  read.
- **Match to need.** Touching distributed systems? Read Kleppmann.
  Drowning in legacy code? Read Feathers. Let your current work pull the
  reading.
- **Primary sources win.** The actual paper, the actual changelog, the
  actual code outrank summaries of them.
- **Revisit.** The good ones reward a second read years later, when
  you've got the experience to see what you missed.
